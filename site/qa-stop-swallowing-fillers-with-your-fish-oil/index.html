Q&A: Stop Swallowing Fillers with your Fish Oil

Published October 12, 2025 by HST Medical (local)

When it comes to fish oil, bigger isn’t always better! Many consumers assume a 1000 mg capsule is automatically superior to a smaller 300 mg mini gel. This is a common misconception, and the difference lies in one key word: concentration. We break down the science, the dosage, and why choosing a high-concentration mini gel is the smart way to get your essential Omega-3s.

Concentration vs. Capsule Size: The Filler Myth

Q: I see a 1000 mg fish oil capsule on the shelf, but it’s huge. Your mini gel only offers 300 mg. Doesn’t that mean I’m getting less?

A: This is where you need to check the fine print! When a capsule says 1000 mg of fish oil, that refers to the total weight of the oil inside. However, the true health benefits come only from the active omega-3 components: EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid) and DHA (docosahexaenoic acid).

A standard 1000 mg capsule may contain only about 300 mg of combined EPA and DHA. The remaining 700 mg is often just filler oil (like lower-quality fish oil or other non-beneficial fats).

Q: So, how are high-concentration mini gels different?

A: High-concentration mini gels, like the one in this formula (providing 180 mg EPA and 120 mg DHA for a total of 300 mg active Omega-3s), use advanced purification to remove the unnecessary filler oil.

  • Standard 1000 mg Capsule: ∼30% active EPA/DHA + ∼70% filler oil.
    Concentrated Mini Gel (300 mg total): ∼100% active EPA/DHA.

Because the filler oil is removed, the capsule becomes much smaller (a mini gel!), making it easier to swallow, and you still get the optimal dose of the ingredients your body actually needs. You’re paying for Omega-3s, not bulk oil.

Omega-3 Dosage and Daily Needs

Q: How much EPA and DHA do I really need in a day for general health?

A: Health organizations generally recommend that healthy adults consume a minimum of 250 mg to 500 mg of combined EPA and DHA daily for general maintenance of brain, heart, and general health. You can get this from eating about two servings of fatty fish (like salmon or sardines) per week. For higher needs (e.g., to support high triglycerides, serious inflammation, or intensive heart health), higher doses (sometimes 1000 mg to 4000 mg combined EPA/DHA) may be recommended

The Power of EPA and DHA: Core Benefits

Q: Why are EPA and DHA so important for my health?

A: EPA and DHA are essential fatty acids, meaning your body can’t produce them efficiently on its own, so you must get them from your diet or supplements. They are vital building blocks for every cell membrane in your body.

Omega-3 Fatty Acid Primary Benefits

EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid)

  • Heart Health: Reduces triglycerides and blood pressure and supports healthy heart rhythm.
  • Inflammation Control: Powerful anti-inflammatory effects help ease joint stiffness and chronic pain.

DHA (docosahexaenoic acid)

  • Brain Health: A major structural component of the brain and eyes, crucial for cognitive function, memory, and mood.
  • Vision: Essential for the maintenance of normal vision.
