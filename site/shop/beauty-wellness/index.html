What is NMN?

NMN (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide) is a naturally occurring molecule found in every cell of our bodies. It’s a precursor to NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide), a coenzyme found in all living cells, that plays a crucial role in energy production, DNA repair, and overall cellular health. It is said that our NAD+ levels would have dived to half that of our younger days (source). It would appear that taking NMN orally converts it to NAD+.

We can find NMN in foods such as edamame, broccoli, cabbage, cucumber, and avocado in small quantities (1 mg per 100 g).

In Chinese medicine, it is believed that pearl powder may have anti-inflammatory, detoxification, and relaxant properties. Glutathione can be produced in our liver and is involved in building and repairing body tissue and plays a part in our immunity (source). Collagen is the structural protein in many of our bodies’ connective tissues, making up between 25% to 35% of our bodies’ proteins.
